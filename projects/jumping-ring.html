Jumping Ring: Height Against Temperature

How the temperature of a ring changes its launch height on a Thomson coil. 링의 온도가 톰슨 코일 위에서의 도약 높이를 어떻게 바꾸는지 측정했습니다.

PHYS 20CL Electromagnetic induction Liquid nitrogen Video analysis

Overview개요

PHYS 20CL final project, Spring 2025. In the jumping-ring demonstration an AC solenoid induces a current in a metal ring, and the ring is thrown upward. I asked how the height of the jump depends on the temperature of the ring.

I launched the ring at four temperatures: −196 °C (liquid nitrogen), −52 °C (freezer), 22 °C (room) and 148 °C (hot plate), with five trials at each. I filmed every jump at 60 frames per second and measured the peak height in Fiji.

The colder the ring, the higher it jumped. A linear fit to the three temperatures I could read with the thermal camera gives −0.232 ± 0.011 cm per °C. The liquid-nitrogen jump was the highest, but it fell about 19 cm below that line, so the straight line holds only between −52 °C and 148 °C. A higher jump from a colder ring is the direction expected if the resistance of the ring falls as it cools, so the induced current grows.
